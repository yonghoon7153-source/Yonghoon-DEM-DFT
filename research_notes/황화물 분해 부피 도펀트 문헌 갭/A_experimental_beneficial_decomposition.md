## Q1. Which experimental papers (2016–2026) dope argyrodites/thiophosphates so that an in situ interphase with a desired product forms? (dopant → claimed product → evidence → electrode side → metric)

### Takeaway
Yes, it's established on the **Li-metal (anode) side**. At least ~20 experimental papers (2018–2026, many groups) put in a dopant or substituent and credit the improvement to a specific in situ product: I→LiI, F→LiF, N→Li3N, O→Li2O/Li3PO4, Cl-rich→LiCl, Cu/Br→LiCl/LiBr, and metals→Li–In/Ga/Sn/Sb/Mg alloys or metallic La⁰/Ta⁰/"Li–Nd". The evidence is almost always post-cycling XPS, sometimes ToF-SIMS, and product amounts are rarely quantified. On the **cathode side** the evidence is much thinner: F→F-rich CEI on LiCoO2, O→Li3PO4-like CEI, Ta→LiTaO3, and Cl content changing which products form. The one experimental "oxidative decomposition is beneficial through Li⁺ release" report found is Kang et al. 2025, and it uses a surface coating, not a dopant.

### Inferences
- **The concept is not new experimentally on the anode side.** "Pick a dopant so the in situ SEI contains LiF / Li3N / LiI / Li2O / Li3PO4 / LiCl, or a lithiophilic alloy" is an established design heuristic, repeated across many groups (C. Wang, X. Sun, L.-Z. Fan, J. Tu, C. Yu, X. Liu (UCAS), IOP CAS, J.-W. Lee). A computational study cannot claim the *concept* as new.
- **What is still missing is quantitative.** No paper found predicts or measures *which products form, in what amount, and with what electronic character*, across dopants under one protocol. Assignments are post hoc XPS binding-energy labels, often on minor components. For example, the In⁰ fraction in [Li2024InF3] is small, and the "Li–Nd alloy" in [Xu2026NdO] is one small peak. A ranking that predicts product identity, phase fraction, gap/σ_e, metallic content, and volume change per dopant would be a different angle from the experiments.
- **Co-doping (In+O, Nd+O, La+O, Y+O, Zn+F, Ga+F, Mg+F) makes single-dopant attribution ambiguous** in the experiments. A computational decomposition of each component's contribution is an available angle.
- **The cathode side is underpopulated.** Dopant-designed CEIs are F→F-rich CEI, O→Li3PO4-like, Ta→LiTaO3, and Cl content changing product quality. A Kang-type benefit (SE oxidation releasing Li⁺ that re-lithiates the cathode) has no dopant-design follow-up found. That looks like the least-occupied experimental space.
- **Wu 2026 already includes a DFT bandgap screening of ~15–16 P-site cation substituents** (Ta, Nb, Mo, W, Ce, La, Fe, Y, Cr, As, Bi, Sb, Si, Ge, Sn, In) before choosing Ta. The project audit says it has no DFT+U or spin, Mo/W come out essentially metallic, and In was computed but left out of the bar chart ([litdb digest](/home/user/Yonghoon-DEM-DFT/litdb/papers/wu2026_ta_argyrodite_selfpassivating.md)). So "computational screening of dopants for self-passivation" has been touched, but only as the bandgap of the doped bulk, not the decomposition-product set.

## Q2. Which papers explicitly use the framing 'self-passivating', 'in situ formed protective interphase', 'sacrificial', 'beneficial decomposition', 'parasitic reaction benefit', 'interphase engineering via composition'?

### Takeaway
The "decomposition-as-design" framing exists in the experimental literature, but it is a minority wording. Explicit cases: "self-passivating" (Wu 2026, title); "benefit of the parasitic reaction" (Kang 2025, title); "stress-self-limiting reaction" / "dynamically stable MCI" (Li 2024); "self-healing" fluorinated interface (Zhao 2020); "in situ formation … enabled by nitrogen doping" (Liu 2021, title); and, in a Na system, deliberately "leverag[ing] the decomposition" of Na3SbS4 (EES 2025). The exact phrases **"beneficial decomposition" and "sacrificial"** were not found for sulfide dopants. Most dopant papers frame their results as stability, compatibility or electronic structure ("electron redistribution", "electronic localization", "orbital hybridization", "p-band center") and then attribute the gain to the products.

- The cleanest **ordinal validation targets** for a computational ranking of dopant-derived interphases are:

## Q5. Any reports that contradict the 'beneficial' interpretation (ionically blocking products, or electronically conducting products such as metallic alloys causing failure)?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the conflict is direct. The classic Janek/Zeier and Roling work shows that reducible tetrel cations (Ge/Si/Sn) give electronically conducting alloy/Li3P-containing MCIs that grow without stopping. Wang 2021 found metal-cation dopants degrade CCD while Si improves it. Burton 2025 shows even plain LPSCl's SEI is not self-limiting: partially lithiated LixP percolation drives growth, and the authors suggest the same mechanism for *any* interphase containing partially lithiated P *or any Li alloy*. Against this, many 2021–2026 dopant papers credit small amounts of metallic or alloy products (Cu, In, Ga, Sn/Sb, La⁰, Ta⁰, "Li–Nd", LiMg, Li–Ge in a composite) as "lithiophilic" and beneficial, usually without quantifying them. Experimental evidence that dopant-derived *insulating* products (LiF/Li3PO4/Li2O) are ionically blocking in sulfide cells was not found.

### Cited Findings
#### Anode side: electronically conducting products → failure (contradicting "alloy = beneficial")
- **LGPS at Li** forms Li3P, Li2S and a Li–Ge alloy, and interfacial resistance rises; growth follows a square-root-of-time law ([Wenzel2016](https://researchhub.com/paper/7525235/direct-observation-of-the-interfacial-instability-of-the-fast-ionic-conductor-lisub10subgepsub2subssub12sub-at-the-lithium-metal-anode)). Below 0.6 V vs Li⁺/Li, Ge alloys with Li to an electronically conductive LixGe; the resulting mixed ion–electron conductor drives continuous degradation and dendrite growth (review-level statement in the [same result set](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2294978050)).
- **MCIs for Ge, Si and Si/Sn LGPS-type SEs**, with electronic conductivity (10⁻⁷–10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹) about equal to ionic, which "enables continuous decomposition"; a tetrele-free glass-ceramic is Li⁺-only — [Bron2017 PeerRef](https://peeref.com/works/4051725); [Marburg thesis](https://open.uni-marburg.de/entities/thesis/c9f4424e-210e-427e-9165-2d38c56f0f23). Citing work generalizes: MCIs are "typically observed in SEs that contain metal cations M⁴⁺ (e.g. M = Ge, Ti), which are then reduced to M⁰" — [RSC EES Batteries 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/eb/d5eb00024f)
- **Metal-cation dopants hurt the interface**: CCD tracks dopant-element resistivity; Mo-type metal cations give lower interface energy and slower interfacial Li transport (dendrites); Si helps — [Wang2021Nano X-MOL](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1363620444838858752); [BL record](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100130342590_0x000001)
- **Even undoped LPSCl's SEI is not passivating**. LPSCl decomposes "at potentials well above full reduction to Li3P", giving partially lithiated LixP. A LixP gradient forms through the interphase. Li diffusion through LixP is proposed as rate-limiting. Growth is predicted to continue "as long as metallic lithium is present and a LixP percolation pathway exists", and the mechanism "could apply to any interphase containing partially lithiated phosphorus, or to any lithium alloy" — [Burton2025 Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:d333957a-408c-4c15-b55f-7a59349d9e5b); [Nature Index](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41467-025-64357-3) [E]
- Authors of in situ-interphase papers themselves state the problem: "in-situ development of mixed ionic-electronic conducting SEI at sulfide electrolyte/Li-metal anode interface induces uneven Li electrodeposition, which causes Li-dendrites and void formation" — [NanoRes2024 PDF](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12274-024-6871-3.pdf). Also, SEI and MCI "significantly block rapid Li-ion transport and induce uneven Li deposition and continuous interface degradation" — [Li2024SMCI record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_2879407850)

### Inferences
- The literature is split on metallic/alloy products. The MCI school (Ge/Si/Sn/Ti; Wang 2021's metal-cation penalty; Burton 2025's percolation criterion) predicts harm. The lithiophilic-MII school (2021–2026 dopant papers; the 2025 review) claims benefit, usually for small, unquantified amounts.
- A computational ranking that predicts the **amount and connectivity (percolation) of electronically conducting products** per dopant could explain both camps. This looks like a clearer novelty angle than "beneficial decomposition" as a concept.
- Burton 2025 implies that even "good" insulating products may not passivate if a LixP or alloy percolation path remains. A product ranking should therefore include a criterion for whether metallic or partially lithiated phases are present and connected, not only the bandgap of each phase.
- On the cathode, the useful contradicting evidence is about *quality vs quantity* (Zuo) and *the onset cannot move* (Banik). A cathode-side dopant ranking should target product identity/resistivity and Li⁺ release, not onset voltage.
