**2.5. Computational Methods**

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed with Quantum ESPRESSO and the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation,[1–4] using SSSP efficiency pseudopotentials (ultrasoft for Li, B, P, S, and Cl; projector augmented-wave (PAW) for O) with kinetic energy cutoffs of 60 and 480 Ry for the wavefunctions and charge density.[5–7] Γ-centered Monkhorst–Pack *k*-point grids[8] and Marzari–Vanderbilt smearing[9] were used; the grids and convergence thresholds of each model are given in the Supporting Information.

Three structural models were constructed: LPSC₁.₆ (Li₂₇P₅S₂₂Cl₈), O-LPSC₁.₆ (Li₂₇P₅S₂₁OCl₈), and BO-LPSC₁.₆ (Li₅₈P₈B₂S₄₁O₃Cl₁₆; one B₂O₃ unit in a 1 × 1 × 2 LPSC₁.₆ supercell). The anion and Li⁺ arrangements and the dopant sites were selected with the Universal Model for Atoms (UMA) machine-learning interatomic potential,[10] and the O site of BO-LPSC₁.₆ was confirmed by DFT. For each model, the ionic positions were relaxed by DFT in isotropically scaled cells, and the energy–volume data were fitted to the third-order Birch–Murnaghan equation of state to obtain the equilibrium volume (*V*₀) and bulk modulus (*B*₀);[11] the structures relaxed at *V*₀ were used in all subsequent analyses.

Li⁺ transport was simulated by UMA-based molecular dynamics (MD) in the *NVT* ensemble with a Langevin thermostat (time step 2 fs) at 600, 800, and 1000 K, using the Atomic Simulation Environment;[12] each run comprised 5 ps of equilibration and 200 ps of production, and at least three independent runs were performed for each model and temperature. The Li⁺ tracer diffusion coefficient (*D*) was obtained from linear fits of the mean square displacement (MSD) over 2–50 ps, the activation energy (*E*ₐ) from an Arrhenius fit of the run-averaged *D*, and the ionic conductivity from the Nernst–Einstein relation with a Haven ratio of 1 and *D* extrapolated to 273 K (Equations S1–S3 in the Supporting Information). Li⁺ migration pathways were mapped with a simplified bond valence site energy (BVSE) approach,[13] in which the site energy of a Li⁺ probe on a ~0.25 Å grid was approximated by the squared deviation of its bond valence sum from unity (Equation S4); the Li⁺ accessible volume fraction was defined as the fraction of grid points within 0.5 (valence units)² of the global minimum.

The density of states (DOS) and projected DOS (PDOS) of BO-LPSC₁.₆ were calculated with the optimized tetrahedron method,[14] with energies referenced to the valence band maximum (VBM) of a fixed-occupation calculation. The electron localization function (ELF)[15] was evaluated at the bond midpoints and averaged over each bond type. Crystal orbital Hamilton population (COHP) analysis[16] was performed with LOBSTER 5.1.1 on PAW wavefunctions (charge spilling 1.19%),[17,18] and the integrated COHP (ICOHP) values up to the Fermi energy were averaged over each bond type. Bond lengths were averaged over first-shell pairs in the *V*₀ structures.

**Computational Details** (Supporting Information)

First-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using Quantum ESPRESSO with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation.[1–4] Pseudopotentials from the SSSP efficiency library (ultrasoft for Li, B, P, S, and Cl; projector augmented-wave (PAW) for O) were used with kinetic energy cutoffs of 60 Ry for the wavefunctions and 480 Ry for the charge density.[5–7] The Brillouin zone was sampled with Γ-centered Monkhorst–Pack *k*-point grids (6 × 6 × 3, 2 × 2 × 1, and 3 × 3 × 1 for LPSC₁.₆, O-LPSC₁.₆, and BO-LPSC₁.₆, respectively),[8] and Marzari–Vanderbilt smearing of 0.01 Ry was applied (0.02 Ry for the structural relaxations of BO-LPSC₁.₆).[9] Ionic positions were relaxed until all force components fell below 10⁻³ Ry bohr⁻¹ (10⁻⁴ Ry bohr⁻¹ for LPSC₁.₆), with a self-consistent field (SCF) convergence threshold of 10⁻⁸ Ry or tighter.

Three structural models were constructed. LPSC₁.₆ (Li₂₇P₅S₂₂Cl₈, i.e., Li₅.₄PS₄.₄Cl₁.₆) was obtained by stacking five primitive argyrodite cells (*a* ≈ 7.0 Å, *α* = 60°) along the *c* axis. Its anion and Li⁺ disorder was sampled with the Universal Model for Atoms (UMA) machine-learning interatomic potential (UMA-s-1p1, OMat task).[10] The 45 distributions of S²⁻ and Cl⁻ over the free-anion (4a/4d) sites were ranked by UMA energy, Li⁺ arrangements were sampled for the lowest-energy anion distributions, and the lowest-energy configuration was annealed by UMA-based molecular dynamics (MD) at 500 K for 50 ps. O-LPSC₁.₆ (Li₂₇P₅S₂₁OCl₈, i.e., Li₅.₄PS₄.₂O₀.₂Cl₁.₆) was obtained by replacing one S²⁻ in LPSC₁.₆ with O²⁻ at the lowest-UMA-energy site among four candidates (one free S²⁻ site and three PS₄ corners). BO-LPSC₁.₆ (Li₅₈P₈B₂S₄₁O₃Cl₁₆, i.e., Li₅.₈P₀.₈B₀.₂S₄.₁O₀.₃Cl₁.₆) was obtained by placing one B₂O₃ unit (two B³⁺ on P⁵⁺ sites, three O²⁻ on S²⁻ sites, and four added Li⁺ for charge balance) in a 1 × 1 × 2 LPSC₁.₆ supercell; the configuration was selected by Ewald pre-screening and UMA relaxation, with the O site confirmed by DFT. Both doped models thus contain the smallest dopant amount each cell allows, more than the experimental samples (0.2 vs 0.045 O and 0.2 vs 0.03 B per formula unit).

For each model, the UMA-optimized cell was isotropically scaled to 0.96–1.06 (LPSC₁.₆, 11 volumes), 0.94–1.06 (O-LPSC₁.₆, 7 volumes), or 0.96–1.04 (BO-LPSC₁.₆, 5 volumes) of its volume, and the ionic positions were relaxed by DFT at each fixed cell. The energy–volume data were fitted to the third-order Birch–Murnaghan equation of state to obtain the equilibrium volume (*V*₀) and bulk modulus (*B*₀),[11] and the ions were finally relaxed at *V*₀; these structures were used in all subsequent analyses. Variable-cell relaxation was not used because a single static configuration of disordered argyrodite does not retain the cubic symmetry of its time-averaged structure.

Li⁺ transport was simulated by UMA-based MD in the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE)[12] in the *NVT* ensemble with a Langevin thermostat (time step 2 fs, friction 0.02 in ASE units) at 600, 800, and 1000 K, with 5 ps of equilibration followed by 200 ps of production. For each model and temperature, at least three independent runs with different random seeds were performed. The Li⁺ tracer diffusion coefficient (*D*) was obtained by fitting Eq. (S1) to the mean square displacement (MSD) over 2 ≤ *t* ≤ 50 ps, where *N* is the number of Li⁺ ions, ***r***ᵢ(*t*) is the unwrapped position of ion *i* at time *t* after the start of production, and *c* is a free intercept. The activation energy (*E*ₐ) was obtained by fitting Eq. (S2) to the run-averaged *D* at the three temperatures. The ionic conductivity (*σ*) was estimated from Eq. (S3) with a Haven ratio of 1, using *D* extrapolated to 273 K by Eq. (S2), where *D*₀ is the pre-exponential factor, *k*<sub>B</sub> is the Boltzmann constant, *T* is the temperature, *n* is the Li⁺ number density, and *q* is the Li⁺ charge.

```latex
\mathrm{MSD}(t) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\boldsymbol{r}_{i}(t) - \boldsymbol{r}_{i}(0)\right|^{2} \approx 6Dt + c \qquad (\mathrm{S}1)
```

```latex
D = D_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{E_{\mathrm{a}}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\right) \qquad (\mathrm{S}2)
```

```latex
\sigma = \frac{n q^{2} D}{k_{\mathrm{B}} T} \qquad (\mathrm{S}3)
```

Li⁺ migration pathways were mapped on the *V*₀ structures with a simplified bond valence site energy (BVSE) approach.[13] On a grid with ~0.25 Å spacing, the bond valence sum of a Li⁺ probe was computed over all anions within 5 Å (*R*₀ = 2.105, 2.249, and 1.466 Å for Li–S, Li–Cl, and Li–O, respectively; *b* = 0.37 Å), and the site energy was approximated by Eq. (S4), where *R* is the Li⁺–anion distance. The Li⁺ accessible volume fraction was defined as the fraction of grid points within 0.5 (valence units)² of the global minimum of the periodic map; for display, the maps were resampled into ~9.9 Å near-cubic boxes.

```latex
E_{\mathrm{BV}} = \left(V_{\mathrm{Li}} - 1\right)^{2}, \quad V_{\mathrm{Li}} = \sum_{X} \exp\left(\frac{R_{0} - R}{b}\right) \qquad (\mathrm{S}4)
```

The density of states (DOS) and projected DOS (PDOS) of BO-LPSC₁.₆ were calculated non-self-consistently on a 7 × 7 × 1 *k*-point grid with the optimized tetrahedron method and projections onto atomic orbitals,[14] and were broadened with a Gaussian of 0.05 eV standard deviation for display. Energies were referenced to the valence band maximum (VBM), which was taken from the Kohn–Sham eigenvalues of a fixed-occupation calculation. The electron localization function (ELF) was calculated from the SCF valence density,[15] and its value at the bond midpoint was averaged over all bonds of each type (bonds identified within 2.15, 1.9, 2.3, 2.9, and 2.95 Å for B–S, P–O, P–S, Li–S, and Li–Cl, respectively). For LPSC₁.₆, the ELF was taken from a separate SCF calculation with SG15 optimized norm-conserving Vanderbilt pseudopotentials and cutoffs of 80 and 320 Ry.[16,17]

Crystal orbital Hamilton population (COHP) analysis[18] was performed with LOBSTER 5.1.1.[19,20] Because LOBSTER requires PAW wavefunctions, the SCF and non-self-consistent (no symmetry, 960 bands) calculations were repeated on the same structure with PAW datasets from PSlibrary,[7] and the wavefunctions were projected onto the pbeVaspFit2015 basis (Li 1*s*, 2*s*, 2*p*; B and O 2*s*, 2*p*; P, S, and Cl 3*s*, 3*p*), with a charge spilling of 1.19%. Integrated COHP (ICOHP) values up to the Fermi energy were averaged over each bond type. Bond lengths were averaged over first-shell pairs in the *V*₀ structures (P–S 1.8–2.5, P–O 1.3–1.9, B–S 1.5–2.2, Li–S 1.9–3.2, and Li–Cl 2.0–3.4 Å).
