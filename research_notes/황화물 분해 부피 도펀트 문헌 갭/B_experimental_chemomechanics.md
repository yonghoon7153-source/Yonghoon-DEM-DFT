## Q1. Which experimental studies directly observe or quantify volume change, swelling or stress from SE decomposition or interphase growth in sulfide ASSBs? What magnitudes were reported?

### Takeaway
Direct, decomposition-specific measurements exist only since about 2021. They are mostly operando stack-pressure studies, which give integrated whole-cell pressure, plus a few XCT/TEM imaging studies.
- **Cathode side.** SE oxidation is reported as volume shrinkage, seen as a cell-pressure drop. Values range from about −40 kPa to −200 kPa, plus one unverified −1.3 MPa. The shrinkage is linked to contact loss.
- **Anode side.** Reduction of Li3PS4 gives only a slight measured pressure rise (+70 kPa). At Li|LSPS, stack pressure falls as the interphase forms.
- **Missing.** No study reports a measured *local* interphase strain (%) for Li6PS5Cl decomposition. All volume-% values in the field are DFT-computed.

### Inferences
- **Sign problem for anyone using "decomposition volume change" as a descriptor.** Measured signs depend on the reference state.
  - Cathode-side oxidation removes Li from the SE, and the measured result is shrinkage (pressure drop). This holds in Li3PS4/VGCF, NCM811|LPSCl and NCM622|LPSCl.
  - Anode-side reduction expands the SE locally (Siller: Li2S + LinP). In a closed cell, however, Li metal is consumed. Li|LSPS shows a pressure *decrease* as the interphase grows (Lee 2021, via the 2026 review). A plausible reading is that the products occupy less volume than SE + consumed Li, while still expanding relative to the SE alone.
  - A DFT screen therefore has to declare: (i) whether the Li reservoir is inside the volume balance (open vs closed for Li); (ii) the normalization (per SE formula unit, per transferred e−, or per reacted SE volume); and (iii) local (interphase/contact) vs cell-level strain. These map onto different experimental observables: local contact loss or fracture vs stack pressure.
- **Cell-level pressure is small and confounded.** Reported SE-attributed pressure changes run from tens of kPa to about 0.2 MPa. One value, −1.3 MPa, is unverified. CAM breathing is about 6% in volume for NCM811 (Xu 2026), and conversion cathodes swing up to 6 MPa. Isolating SE decomposition needs control cells: LTO counter electrodes (Xu 2026), SE–carbon "cathodes" (Siller), or three-electrode pressiometry (Oh). A screen's predicted numbers would be checked against these controls, not raw full-cell pressure.
- For argyrodite at the Li anode, the experimental mechanical-failure literature is dominated by filaments and voids (Lee 2021, Xu 2026, the UCL thesis). Interphase-expansion-driven fracture is directly imaged only for Li3PS4 (Dixit), LGPS (Madsen, Davis) and the oxide LAGP (see Q2).

### Gaps
- Lee 2021 pressure magnitudes, initial stack pressure and LSPS/LPSCl densities: the full text is behind pubs.acs.org (not fetchable). The direction "decrease with interphase formation" comes from a secondary summary only.
- The −1.3 MPa (Xu 2026) and −200 kPa (Siller) values: the snippets were truncated. Which SE layer, which counter electrode and what initial pressure apply could not be confirmed.
- No measured local interphase strain (%) or swelling for Li6PS5Cl reduction or oxidation was found. Queries: "operando pressure measurement sulfide solid electrolyte decomposition volume expansion Li6PS5Cl"; "operando pressure decrease first charge sulfide electrolyte oxidation NCM Li6PS5Cl…"; "\"1.3 MPa\" pressure drop LPSC oxidation…".
- No electrochemical dilatometry study isolating SE decomposition in a sulfide cell was found. Dilatometry appears only in Zhang 2017 (electrode breathing).
- No in situ TEM quantification of interphase volume at an argyrodite/cathode interface was found. In situ TEM appears only in Dixit 2020, for Li3PS4 at Li.

## Q2. Which studies attribute contact loss or cracking specifically to SE decomposition (as opposed to cathode lattice breathing or Li plating)? What are the sulfide-specific analogues of Koerver 2017 and Tippens 2019?

### Takeaway
Clean attribution of cracking to the *volume change of the interphase* is established for the oxide LAGP (Tippens 2019, with Lewis 2019 as a companion). For sulfides, the analogues are:
- **Li3PS4/Li.** Interphase pores drive edge fracture (Dixit 2020).
- **LGPS/Li.** Mechanical decomposition was imaged by XCT (Madsen 2020); the onset of mechanical degradation is delayed by ALD (Davis 2020).
- **Argyrodite/NCM (chemical reaction alone).** Cracks and contact loss after 40 h at SOC 0 with no cycling (Jung 2019).
- **Argyrodite/NCM (oxidation).** Contact loss from LPSC volume shrinkage (NCM622 preprint 2024; Xu 2026).

Koerver 2017 itself assigns contact loss to CAM contraction and assigns the interphase to resistance, not to mechanics. For Li6PS5Cl at Li metal, observed cracking is attributed to Li plating, filaments or strain, not to interphase volume.

### Inferences
- **Cathode side.** For argyrodites, the decomposition-specific contact-loss evidence is (a) *chemical* reaction with NCM, without cycling (Jung 2019), and (b) *oxidative shrinkage* of LPSC at carbon/NCM contacts (NCM622 preprint; Xu 2026; Li3PS4 in Siller). Both are about **shrinkage or product formation that opens gaps**, not expansion that cracks. Koerver 2017's widely cited "chemomechanics" is CAM contraction, not SE decomposition. Note this when citing it as precedent.
- **Anode side.** The mechanism "interphase grows with expanded volume → stress at the interphase edge → SE fracture" is shown cleanly only for LAGP. For thiophosphates, the sulfide evidence (Dixit, Madsen, Davis) shows the *link* between interphase and fracture, through pores, current focusing and Li filling of cracks. It does not show the volume-strain mechanism as cleanly.
- For Li6PS5Cl specifically, no study was found that attributes anode-side cracking to SEI volume. ## Q3. Which continuum / FEM / phase-field models include interphase-growth strain or decomposition-induced volumetric strain for sulfide SEs? What inputs did they use for the volume change, and where did those numbers come from?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of models carry a decomposition or interphase volume term.
- **Tian et al. JES 2020 (FEM).** The decomposition volume change is taken from DFT, using the products of the most favorable reaction at the Li chemical potential. The confirmed numbers are for LiPON (about −27%; up to 26.8 GPa).
- **Tippens 2019 (FE).** LAGP, an oxide.
- **Xin Li group (constrained-ensemble thermodynamics).** Reaction strain ε = (V_products − V_SE)/V_SE comes from DFT or database molar volumes and is applied to LGPS/LSPS/LPSCl. It treats the strain as an energy penalty that stabilizes the SE, not as a damage driver.

Mainstream sulfide composite-cathode FEM/phase-field models (Bucci 2017, Taghikhani 2025, grain-level models) drive stress with CAM intercalation strain only. CAM volumes come from experiment (Koerver 2018 partial molar volumes). **No continuum model of an argyrodite cathode or anode with a decomposition-strain term was found.**

The modeled strain is therefore an upper or limiting estimate, which a screen should state.
- **Magnitude cross-check (inference, not a published mapping).** Bucci's ≤7.5% CAM ΔV criterion, at sulfide-like Gc, is the only quantitative crack threshold in a sulfide-parameterized composite model. Decomposition strains of tens of percent (LSPS up to 56%; LiPON −27%) are far above it. Whether that matters depends on interphase thickness, about 3 nm in the NCM622/LPSCl case. Tian shows stress growing with interphase thickness (10 → 50 nm: 9.90 → 26.8 GPa in LiPON).
- **Open niche for modeling.** No sulfide composite-cathode FEM/phase-field model couples (i) CEI growth kinetics, (ii) the *negative* oxidative strain of the SE and (iii) contact-loss criteria. Experimental pressure data (Siller, Xu 2026) now exist to calibrate such a model.

## Q4. Has any experimental or modeling work compared SE compositions or dopants with respect to decomposition-induced mechanical degradation (e.g., Cl-rich vs Cl-poor argyrodite, O-doped, halide-substituted, glass vs crystalline)?

### Takeaway
**Partially established.** Composition dependence of the *decomposition reaction strain* already exists:
- Cl content in Li6−xPS5−xCl1+x (Gil-González 2022);
- Si content in Li-Si-P-S core-shell (Wu 2018);
- LSPS (Wu 2018).

But in that Xin Li line the strain is a *stabilizing* constraint penalty that widens the ESW, not a predictor of cracking or contact loss.

Experimental comparisons touching mechanics are few:
- LSPS vs LPSCl stack-pressure signatures (Lee 2021);
- iodine-doped LPS interphase pores and fracture (Dixit 2020);
- alloy vs Li anodes (Jeong 2026);
- Li6PS5X X = Cl/Br/I interphase growth, chemical/resistance only (Wenzel 2018).

**No study was found that ranks argyrodite dopants (O, Nd, B, Ga/F, Zr/F …) by decomposition volume change as a mechanical-degradation descriptor.**

Recent reviews treat SE decomposition and mechanical failure as coupled, but **qualitatively**. They point to two open problems: electrolyte-level electro-chemo-mechanical degradation (as distinct from CAM–SE detachment) is under-studied, and failure modes in cells are hard to separate.
