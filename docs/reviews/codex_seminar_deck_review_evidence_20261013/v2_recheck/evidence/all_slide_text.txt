## Slide 4
From the contact network to Li⁺ transport
Kirchhoff network: each SE–SE contact = bulk resistance + Holm/Maxwell constriction using Hertz contact radii; σ₀ = 1.5 mS cm⁻¹ [8].
Transport tortuosity T = φ_SE σ₀ / σ_eff: 7.2 → 5.0 (0:10 → 7:3). Contact constriction accounts for 73–75 % of Joule dissipation in this network model.

## Slide 5
What a fixed-shape soft-sphere DEM cannot do
No shape change and no yield cap: overlap is the only plasticity proxy; the softened SE modulus (1.35 vs ≈ 22 GPa) is an effective parameter, not a property [1, 7].
In one 7:3 bed, global viscous damping carries 58 % of the plate reaction during compression — an open issue.
DEM

· global damping, dry compaction: none
< Contact-law illustration from the implementation [1, 7] >
NOTES
Sources: docs/dem_governing_equations_background_20261011.md (§5.3 figure 2; §10.3 literature counts; §11 limits). Damping share 57.9 % = measured on one 7:3 bed during compression (ledger DEMP-01, open). Literature count = selected canonical cards, our classification; not a systematic review. Bulk modulus reference ≈ 22 GPa = our DFT E_VRH 22.06 GPa (literature DEM input 22.1 GPa, Bazzoun 2026 [8]); the glass-electrolyte value 24 GPa (Sakuda 2013 [15]) is a different material system. The statement is about this implementation (fixed spherical shapes, hooke/hysteresis), not about DEM in general.

## Slide 9
Voxelizing the compacted microstructure into a transport mesh
Finite-volume conservation on the phase grid gives electrical (σ, φ) and thermal (κ, T) transport on the same structure.
Faces are joined by a harmonic mean with no interface-resistance term: an ideal-interface voxel model — an upper bound only within the same geometry, properties and boundaries.
