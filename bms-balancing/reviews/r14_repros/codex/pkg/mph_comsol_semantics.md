## Q1: cEeqref, particle SOC, and the reaction OCP

**The generic particle identity is correct, but A1 combines that identity with an additional, model-specific claim about the OCP argument.**

- COMSOL 6.4's theory defines `soc = cs/cs,max`; it calls `cs,max` the total concentration of reaction sites and writes the free-site concentration as `cs,max - cs`. Thus the proposed rebuttal “maximum concentration is unrelated to site count” does not work for the standard insertion model. The same theory distinguishes this local particle quantity from whole-cell SOC and distinguishes full host capacity from usable voltage-window capacity. [Lithium-ion theory](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem_battery.06.63.html); the formula is also text-extracted on printed p.144 of the [6.4 Battery Design guide](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/BatteryDesignModuleUsersGuide.pdf).
- The Particle Intercalation settings define `cs,max` as the maximum intercalated concentration, use it in Lithium Insertion kinetics, and also use it for host capacity when SOC and Initial Charge Distribution is enabled. Importantly, that node's **own** Equilibrium Potential section serves particle transport and heat-of-mixing options. [Particle Intercalation, Species Settings / Equilibrium Potential](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem_battery.06.47.html).
- COMSOL explicitly identifies material-property `cEeqref` as maximum lithium concentration in an official lithium-ion example, while the generic material API table calls `ElectrodePotential.cEeqref` a reference concentration. The XML name is therefore meaningful but its namespace and active source selector matter. [Official 1D thermal model, Materials](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.battery.li_battery_1d_for_thermal_models/li_battery_1d_for_thermal_models.html); [material API properties](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_api_fileformats.53.15.html).
- Reaction kinetics use the equilibrium potential configured in **Porous Electrode Reaction**, which links to settings allowing `From material`, Nernst, or `User defined`. This is a separate consumption path from simply setting the particle's maximum concentration. [Porous Electrode Reaction](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem_battery.06.48.html); [Electrode Reaction, Equilibrium Potential](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem.07.038.html).
- A concrete supported alternative exists: COMSOL's 6.4 1D example selects User defined OCP and explicitly calls `Eeq_neg(liion.cs_surface/csmax_neg)`. A user-defined function can normalize with its own parameter, or consume concentration directly. Hence `pin1.cEeqref` alone cannot establish which denominator the OCP function receives. [1D Isothermal Lithium-Ion Battery, negative Porous Electrode Reaction settings](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.battery.li_battery_1d/li_battery_1d.html).

**Required model evidence:** for each selected electrode, inspect the active `cEeqref` source selector, initial-concentration mode, active `per1` equilibrium-potential source/expression, selected material, material property/function expression and its model-input mapping, and any relevant equation overrides. A From material label plus a table of SOC-versus-OCP points still needs the mapping from the physics concentration to that table's argument. No official source found in this bounded review proves that every From material OCP necessarily uses a user-overridden `pin1.cEeqref` rather than the material's own concentration normalization.

**Implication for M1:** if `csinit=x*csmax` is the actual initialization and `pin1.cEeqref=dm*csmax` is the effective particle maximum, particle occupancy is `x/dm`. If both electrode fraction and effective site density scale by the same remaining fraction, total nominal host sites scale by its square (more exactly `(1-LAM)*dm` with the supplied rounded values). This supports A2/A3 conditionally. It does not prove the computed -14.27 mV OCP difference applies to the active reaction, nor prove the design was unintended. Calling `1-(1-LAM)^2` “effective LAM” must mean nominal host-site reduction, not measured cell capacity loss under voltage cutoffs.

## Q4: Porous Conductive Binder and the 3.3 factor

**Electronic conductivity correction does apply to the documented Porous Conductive Binder feature.** Its official page expressly includes reduction of electrode conductivity by phase fractions/tortuosity and provides Effective Transport Parameter Correction for both electrode and electrolyte. `epss` is the electrode-phase fraction; `epsl` is the electrolyte fraction. Thus the objection “a binder node cannot apply porous correction to electronic conductivity” is not supported. [Porous Conductive Binder](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem_battery.06.51.html).

COMSOL separately documents electrode-volume-fraction use under Bruggeman/Tortuosity in porous electrodes. An official battery example gives the solid-phase form `sigma_eff = sigma*epsilon^gamma`, with gamma=1.5 for its packed spherical-particle case. [Porous Electrode](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.echem/echem_ug_electrochem.06.006.html); [1D Ni-Cd battery](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.battery.nicd_battery_1d/nicd_battery_1d.html).

**Limit:** the publicly accessible binder setting page does not itself spell out the exponent, and no public 6.4 documentation mapping the internal `ElectricCorrModel` token to every active/default coefficient was located. Therefore verify the active binder exponent/correction expression before treating 1.5 as model-specific proof. With exponent 1.5 and `epss=0.45`, the arithmetic is exact: `sigma_eff/sigma_short = 0.3018691769624716` and `sigma_short/sigma_eff = 3.3126933`.

This is a nominal-to-effective conversion, not proof the sweep was mislabeled: a sweep intentionally reporting intrinsic `sigma_short` is coherent. “Copy/paste” is an inference about author intent. Under an otherwise purely resistive homogeneous binder model, only `sigma_short*epss^gamma` is identifiable from that conduction path, but the broader model need not be degenerate if epss also affects other active processes. If there is **no correction**, the reported product degeneracy does not survive as written: epss instead disappears from that conductivity path.

## Q6: disabling socicd1 is necessary but not a complete initialization audit

- If SOC and Initial Charge Distribution is active, Particle Intercalation's direct initial concentration setting becomes inactive: COMSOL derives an initial electrode potential internally and uses an equilibrium-concentration function. The SOC node applies this initialization to the particles included in its positive/negative electrode selections. [Particle Intercalation, Species Settings](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem_battery.06.47.html); [SOC and Initial Charge Distribution](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.battery/battery_ug_electrochem_battery.06.58.html).
- A node genuinely disabled in the main Model Builder cannot be re-enabled in the study-selection tree. Do **not** invent a hidden study re-enable of such a node. Conversely, `Disable in Solvers`/unchecked `Solve for` differs from fully disabled: variables can remain generated, with values provided elsewhere. [Physics and Variables Selection](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.014.html).
- Even with socicd1 fully disabled, Study > Values of Dependent Variables can initialize from another solution rather than the physics initial expression. Physics-controlled selection may itself use an earlier study-step solution. [Common Study Step Settings](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.012.html).
- Solver > Dependent Variables can be user-defined independently of study synchronization, selecting Initial expression or Solution. Its Field subnodes control which variables are solved; a field not solved takes values from the parent's Values of Variables Not Solved For. Field scaling is **not** a direct override of the physical initial concentration. [Dependent Variables](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.130.html); [Field](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.180.html).
- The usual Current Distribution Initialization step solves potentials only. Its mere existence is not evidence that particle concentrations were rebalanced or that the quoted direct concentrations were changed. [General Current Distribution Problems](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.echem/echem_ug_electrochem.06.157.html).

**Conclusion for the main review:** the request supplies a credible, conditional concentration/site-count inconsistency. It does not close all paths required to turn that into a demonstrated OCP shift in the particular active model, an unintended implementation bug, or a prediction about its stored solution. Conversely, “COMSOL was never run” alone is not a refutation: a sufficiently complete static audit can prove selected equations and their arithmetic.
